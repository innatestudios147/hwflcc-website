Three directions for the homepage

Each version uses the same logo, colours, words and photographs, and the same "Does your project fit?" checker. What changes is the type, the layout and how the page moves. Open each one on a computer and on your phone, and scroll all the way down.

These are homepages only. Links to other pages open the current live site.

A

The sentence

The headline “The wind works for Hopefield” splits in two, and the wind farm’s photographs pass through the gap as you scroll.

Feel
Calm and editorial. Mostly white, with colour saved for the four funding areas.
Type
Big Shoulders, a tall condensed face, over Public Sans.
Look for
The photo frame in the headline, the figures that count up, the funding areas as colour fields, “Wind meets fynbos” with photos between the words, and the whole lime “Apply” band as one button.
Trade-off
The quietest of the three. Its best moment needs a wide screen.

B

The poster

The town’s name is a band the wind keeps pushing across the page, passing over a window onto the turbines.

Feel
Loud and flat, like a printed poster. The most colour and the biggest type.
Type
Red Hat Display Black in capitals, over Red Hat Text.
Look for
The moving “Hopefield” band, “Our wind” held still while photos pass, the steps as three posters (Check, Prepare, Send), a wall of fact tiles, and the name in coloured blocks in the footer.
Trade-off
The boldest and most memorable, and the busiest. The name keeps moving (it pauses under the mouse and stops for visitors who turn motion off).

C

Colour chapters

Each funding area owns one brand colour, and the whole page is built from those four colours: Education deep blue, Social welfare green, Health lime and Development sky blue.

Feel
Organised and warm. The closest to a finished organisation website.
Type
One face, Hanken Grotesk, in two weights.
Look for
The masthead of photos and colour tiles, the 50 km map with the time in Hopefield right now, the four colour chapters, the five steps as panels that open, and the footer made of tiles.
Trade-off
The least surprising. The easiest to carry across every page of the site.
Open version C

In all three

  • The Hopefield logo and the brand’s blue, green and lime, set so every text colour passes accessibility contrast.
  • Only facts from the current site: no invented quotes, partners or figures.
  • The five photographs from the current gallery. No stock images.
  • “Does your project fit?”: four questions that check a project against the funding rules and list the documents to send.
  • Only the postal address for now. The contact email stays off the site until the new address is confirmed.
  • Movement that switches off for visitors who turn motion off on their device.

Choose A, B or C, or a mix (for example B’s opening with C’s funding chapters). The chosen style then goes onto every page of the site.
